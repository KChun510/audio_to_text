Writing as an engineer or scientist requires skills beyond general writing. Although you have taken general writing courses from middle school to high school to first year English, you still need additional skills to write as an engineer or scientist. One skill is how to target readers with distinctly different levels of knowledge about your content. From experts in your field to engineers and scientists outside your discipline to those who do not even study engineering or science. Another skill concerns how to organize a report, how to write a proper title, how to write a summary that presents up front the report's most valuable details, how to position secondary details into appendices.

and how to divide the report's main text into sections with an understanding that audiences have specific expectations for certain sections, such as an introduction. At the sentence level, you need to realize that the main goal is not to be ornate or flowery, but as Albert Einstein said, to keep things as simple as possible, yet no simpler. To support your assertions, you need to know how to design and incorporate figures, tables, and equations. All other skills are adhering to sophisticated formats, writing as part of a team, and realizing when you should be meticulous and when you should provide a big picture.
